A bridge to atomistic dynamics.

I want to explore machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) as a way to connect my computational chemistry background with questions about energy materials and electrochemical interfaces.

My experience includes oxygen-vacancy studies in perovskite oxides at the University of Ulm and thermodynamic calculations of Prussian blue cathode materials at DGIST. These experiences motivate an interest in moving from selected atomistic configurations toward their evolution over time.

An emerging interest: I have not yet defined a specific MLIP project. The questions below are possible directions I hope to refine during doctoral research, not completed projects or established results.

01 / Defects & local structure

Could a carefully validated potential help explore how oxygen-vacancy arrangements affect local structure and atomic motion in an oxide?

02 / Interfacial environments

What could atomistic simulations reveal about solvent and ion organization near an electrode? Which effects would require explicit treatment of charge and electrode potential?

03 / Learning where it matters

How should reference configurations be selected so a potential remains reliable along the trajectories and environments relevant to a particular chemical question?

What would make the study convincing?

I would want to define a focused material system and observable, choose an appropriate treatment of its chemistry, compare against independent reference calculations, and test behavior beyond the training configurations. For reactive or electrochemical systems, a generic potential should not be assumed to describe bond changes, charge transfer, or applied potential without specific validation.

Why this connects to my other interests

My battery inference work asks which measurements provide useful information. An MLIP project could ask a related question at the atomic scale: which configurations provide the most useful reference information for the chemical behavior we want to understand?

Future research interest informed by my computational chemistry experience. These prospective questions are starting points for discussion.
